#4471 · three bugs, not one

owner Paweł Pomianek open reviewed 2026-10-28 · 0 days ago

#4471 was merged as one bug, marked known, and closed on 5 October. The queue it was built from holds three. Engineering’s update covers two of them.

One bug is still open. It was never a ticket of its own inside #4471, so closing #4471 did not reach it, and the parser fix does not touch it.

Where the desk got to

Engineering closed #4471 on 5 October with two outcomes, and both are right. The parser fix shipped: fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales. The hint bug was marked won’t fix, because booking-v3 is retired and a retired form cannot print the wrong hint again. Those two tickets are closed here too.

The third was not in the update. Inside #4471 it was never a bug — it was five reports counted under the parser with a footnote under the table — so the close passed over it. The zone is still wrong. Ticket #3 stays open.

Why the desk saw one bug

Nobody misread anything. Three things each hid part of the queue, and each of them is working as designed.

Merging on subject. Decision 0001 merges a report into an open ticket when the subject matches, so nobody has to read the body. Twelve subject lines cover all of these reports, and every one of them says the same thing: the day is wrong. Three bugs share those twelve subjects, so three bugs share one ticket. The decision names this as a consequence and accepts it — engineering merges them later. Nobody did.

The tag is a guess. Intake tags a report from the reporter’s browser the moment it arrives. The cause depends on the tenant: the locale they write dates in, and the booking form they run. Those two are not the same question, and they disagree often.

  • Intake's tag disagrees with the cause on 38 of 412 reports (9%). Intake reads the browser; the cause follows the tenant.
  • The export holds 427 rows for 412 reports: 15 ids come twice, with the same content. Count rows and you overstate the queue.

A footnote is not a ticket. The page for #4471 renders its causes from the same data this page does, and it has been printing the second bug on its own row, labelled not this ticket — no ticket of its own, since it was written. It also footnotes five reports as a one-day move and then counts them under the parser anyway, because causes.ts has no rule that can see a one-day move. The table was right. It was read as a table about one bug.

The three

The shared parser reads month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first, so dates come out with day and month swapped: 7 April goes out as 4 July. This is the bug #4471 names, and the fix that shipped is for this one.

booking-v3 prints the wrong hint. Its form printed MM/DD/YYYY under the date field. Its tenants are day-first, so their people typed what the hint told them, month first, and the form read it the tenant’s way. The date was wrong before the shared parser saw it — which is why fixing the parser would not have closed these 33 reports, and why retiring the form does.

Five invites move by one day. A month flip cannot move a date by one day. These five describe a zone, not a swap: one meeting held twice because Lisbon got Tuesday and Toronto got Monday, one tenant off by a day in June and no other month, one invite that arrived before the booking was made. The desk had already read them and written the ids down. They need the booking’s zone carried with the booking, and the DST boundaries checked. Nothing in the 5 October update does either.

What we changed in the kb

Nothing. #4471 stands as the desk’s record of the queue, and decision 0001 stands as accepted — the kb’s own rule is that an accepted decision is replaced, never edited. This page is new, and the rule that splits the queue is new: src/lib/kb/split-4471.ts adds the one-day group that causes.ts cannot see, and imports causes.ts for the rest rather than editing it. Every number on this page is computed from the export at render time.

Lessons

  • Merging on subject counts a queue. It does not tell you how many bugs are in it. One of those is what engineering needs.
  • A ticket closing is not the bugs inside it closing. #4471 is closed and one of its bugs is still shipping.
  • A table that prints no ticket of its own is asking for a ticket. Read what the page renders, not only what it says.
  • Intake’s tag is a guess from the browser. Where it disagrees with the tenant, the tenant wins.
  • When the desk reads a report differently from the rule, the note belongs in the rule, not under the table.
